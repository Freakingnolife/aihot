## TCT: ORNL and Boeing WAAM-print 2-ton stamp form die for NASA HiCAM

Publisher: **TCT Magazine** · Original date: 2026-09-29T09:14:54.000Z · Backfill: **yes**

TCT Magazine reported that Oak Ridge National Laboratory and The Boeing Company partnered to additively manufacture a 2-ton Stamp Form Die mould using ORNL's Arc-1 wire-arc additive manufacturing system. The steel mould, intended for Boeing's contribution to NASA's Hi-Rate Composite Aircraft Manufacturing project, used mild steel structurally and stainless steel at the surface, with temporary ribs and simulations addressing warping. Baker Industries annealed and finished it. Partners said the technology could be replicated for similar tool types.

## Carbon reports Sherpa Design and MBARI used Carbon DLS with 5-axis CNC for an AUV fluid manifold

Publisher: **Carbon** · Original date: 2026-07-07T16:00:51.000Z · Backfill: **yes**

Carbon states that MBARI needed a custom toroidal fluid manifold with 60 dual fluid ports for its Environmental Sample Processor, after earlier 3D-printed iterations showed brittleness, temperature-extreme performance limits and incompatibility with the cleaning protocol, which used water containing 10% bleach. MBARI explored metal additive manufacturing but cited cost and complexity, then engaged Sherpa Design through Carbon's partner network. Sherpa used Carbon DLS printing with EPX 150 plus post-process 5-axis CNC machining; the part met dimensional requirements and passed pressure testing to 500 psi without leakage. Carbon says the manifold is deployed in the ESP and is currently undergoing field trials aboard MBARI’s LRAUV.

**Editorial judgment:** The hybrid printing-and-machining account is a useful starting point for investigating manifold manufacture; the source’s test does not establish suitability for another part.
